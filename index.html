ClassMaker

The most meaningful part is simple: my own former school now uses something I built.

ClassMaker places roughly 1,000 students across 9 grades and 55 classrooms while respecting capacity, friend requests, teacher restrictions, support-hour caps, and class balance. I also built the spreadsheet import around student IDs so updates don’t create duplicate records. It started as a class-placement problem and became a lesson in translating real human constraints into software.

Personal AI Agent

I wanted an AI that could actually do things for me—not just talk about them.

I’m building an agent that uses MCP, browser automation, and APIs to handle tasks like email, reservations, and web research. I’m also designing an iMessage-style interface for people who don’t want to use a terminal. The hard part is working out what to trust it with while dealing with Claude’s limitations, compute costs, and the memory needed to store and retrieve useful personal context.

Medical Imaging

At Columbia, I got to push my computer-vision work into a much more research-driven setting.

I developed a U-Net semantic-segmentation model to identify individual cell bodies in microscopy images under Columbia BME Professor Andrew Laine. It taught me how different research feels from product work: more ambiguity, more experimentation, and a lot more comfort with results that do not behave the way you expect.

PneumoScan

This project taught me not to underestimate what I could build just because I had never built anything like it before.

I trained a CNN to detect pneumonia in chest X-rays, built a Flask website around it, and kept pushing through everything I did not know yet. It ended up reaching 85% accuracy and placing 2nd in the Congressional App Challenge. More than the award, it gave me confidence that I could teach myself enough to turn an ambitious idea into something real.

A little about me

I’m ambitious, but mostly because I’m curious.

I grew up in Brooklyn and now study Computer Science at Columbia Engineering. I’ve always liked making things—before code, that often meant painting; now it usually means opening too many tabs, trying an idea, breaking it, and figuring out why.

I’m drawn to AI because it feels unusually open-ended. There are still so many products, interfaces, and systems that haven’t been built yet. I want to be around people who are ambitious enough to try.

I care a lot about getting better. The projects here are really just snapshots of that process.
